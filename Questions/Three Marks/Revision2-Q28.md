Chapter: Amines

28. i) Amino group is o- and p-directing in aromatic electrophilic substitution reactions, but aniline on nitration gives a substantial amount of m-nitroaniline. Give reason.
ii) Give a chemical test to distinguish between CH₃NH₂ and (CH₃)₂NH.
iii) Convert Aniline to 4-bromoaniline

## Answer

(i) Nitration of Aniline:
In strongly acidic nitration medium (conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄), aniline is protonated to form the anilinium ion [C₆H₅NH₃⁺]. The –NH₃⁺ group is strongly electron-withdrawing and meta-directing, resulting in 47% meta-nitroaniline alongside 51% para-nitroaniline.

(ii) Distinguishing Test: Carbylamine Test
Primary amine (CH₃NH₂) reacts with chloroform and ethanolic KOH on heating to form offensive-smelling methyl isocyanide (CH₃NC). Secondary amine [(CH₃)₂NH] does not give this test:
CH₃NH₂ + CHCl₃ + 3 KOH(alc) --[heat]--> CH₃NC↑ + 3 KCl + 3 H₂O

(iii) Convert Aniline to 4-bromoaniline:
Step 1: Protect the amino group by acetylating aniline with acetic anhydride in pyridine to form acetanilide:
C₆H₅NH₂ + (CH₃CO)₂O --[pyridine]--> C₆H₅NHCOCH₃ + CH₃COOH
Step 2: Brominate acetanilide with Br₂ in ethanoic acid to get 4-bromoacetanilide:
C₆H₅NHCOCH₃ + Br₂ --[CH₃COOH]--> 4-Br–C₆H₄–NHCOCH₃ + HBr
Step 3: Hydrolyze with acid/base to yield 4-bromoaniline:
4-Br–C₆H₄–NHCOCH₃ + H₂O --[H⁺ or OH⁻]--> 4-Br–C₆H₄–NH₂ + CH₃COOH
