Chapter: Chemical Kinetics

24. (a) The rate of a reaction quadruples when the temperature changes from 293 K to 313 K. Calculate the energy of activation of the reaction assuming that it does not change with temperature. (log 4 = 0.6021, R = 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹)
(OR)
(b) For a reaction, A + B → Product, the rate law is given by, Rate = k [A]¹ [B]².
(A) What is the order of the reaction?
(B) Write the unit of rate constant 'k' for the above reaction.
(C) For the reaction A → B, the rate of reaction becomes twenty-seven times when the concentration of A is increased three times. What is the order of reaction?

## Answer

(a) Given:
T₁ = 293 K, T₂ = 313 K, k₂ / k₁ = 4, R = 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹

Formula:
log (k₂ / k₁) = (Eₐ / 2.303 R) × [(T₂ - T₁) / (T₁ × T₂)]

Substitution:
0.6021 = [Eₐ / (2.303 × 8.314)] × [(313 - 293) / (293 × 313)]
0.6021 = (Eₐ / 19.147) × (20 / 91709)
Eₐ = (0.6021 × 19.147 × 91709) / 20 = 52,841 J mol⁻¹ = 52.84 kJ mol⁻¹

Answer:
Eₐ = 52.84 kJ mol⁻¹

(OR)

(b) (A) Order of reaction = 1 + 2 = 3 (Third order).
(B) Unit of k = L² mol⁻² s⁻¹ (or mol⁻² L² s⁻¹).
(C) Rate = k [A]ⁿ. Given 27 = 3ⁿ => 3³ = 3ⁿ => n = 3 (Third order).
