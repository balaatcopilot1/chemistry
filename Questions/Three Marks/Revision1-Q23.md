Chapter: Biomolecules

23. (A) Why sucrose is called invert sugar?
(B) Define glycosidic linkage.
(C) Write any two difference between amylose and amylopectin

## Answer

(a) Invert Sugar:
Sucrose is dextrorotatory (+66.5°). Upon acid or enzymatic hydrolysis, it yields an equimolar mixture of dextrorotatory D-glucose (+52.7°) and laevorotatory D-fructose (-92.4°). Since the resulting mixture is net laevorotatory (-19.85°), the optical rotation inverts from (+) to (-).

(b) Glycosidic Linkage:
An oxide linkage formed by loss of a water molecule that joins two monosaccharide units through an oxygen atom in disaccharides or polysaccharides.

(c) Differences between Amylose and Amylopectin:
1. Amylose is a linear unbranched polymer of α-D-glucose with C1–C4 glycosidic bonds; amylopectin is a branched polymer with C1–C4 linear bonds and C1–C6 branch points.
2. Amylose is water-soluble (15-20% of starch); amylopectin is water-insoluble (80-85% of starch).
