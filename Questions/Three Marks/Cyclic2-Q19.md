Chapter: Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers

19. How does Lucas Reagent help in distinguishing between primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols? Describe the observations made at room temperature.

## Answer

Lucas reagent is a solution of anhydrous zinc chloride (ZnCl₂) in concentrated hydrochloric acid (HCl). It reacts with alcohols to form insoluble alkyl chlorides, producing turbidity in the solution.

Observations at room temperature:

(a) Tertiary (3°) Alcohols:
Produce turbidity immediately at room temperature because tertiary carbocations form rapidly.
Example: (CH₃)₃C–OH + HCl --[anhydrous ZnCl₂]--> (CH₃)₃C–Cl(s) [turbidity] + H₂O

(b) Secondary (2°) Alcohols:
Produce turbidity within 5 minutes at room temperature.
Example: CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₃ + HCl --[anhydrous ZnCl₂]--> CH₃–CH(Cl)–CH₃(s) [turbidity] + H₂O

(c) Primary (1°) Alcohols:
Do not produce turbidity at room temperature. Turbidity appears only upon heating.
Example: CH₃–CH₂–OH + HCl --[anhydrous ZnCl₂]--> No turbidity at room temperature.

*Source: NCERT, Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers*
