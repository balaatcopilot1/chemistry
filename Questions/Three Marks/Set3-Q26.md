Chapter: Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

26. Give reasons for the following :
(a) Although chlorine is an electron-withdrawing group, yet it is ortho-, para-directing in electrophilic aromatic substitution reactions.
(b) Aryl halides cannot be prepared by reacting phenol with concentrated halogen acids or phosphorus halides.
(c) Chloroform is stored in closed dark coloured bottles.

## Answer

(a) Chlorine exerts a strong electron-withdrawing inductive (-I) effect, but its unshared electron pairs delocalize into the aromatic ring via resonance (+R effect). Resonance increases electron density at ortho and para positions relative to meta position, directing electrophiles to o- and p- positions.
(b) In phenol, oxygen lone pairs resonate with the benzene ring, imparting partial double bond character to the C–O bond. This makes the C–O bond very strong and resistant to cleavage by halogen acids (HX) or phosphorus halides (PX₃/PX₅).
(c) Chloroform (CHCl₃) is slowly oxidized by atmospheric oxygen in the presence of light to form poisonous phosgene gas (carbonyl chloride, COCl₂). Storing it in closed, dark bottles excludes air and light to prevent oxidation: 2 CHCl₃ + O₂ → 2 COCl₂ + 2 HCl.

*Source: NCERT, Haloalkanes and Haloarenes*
