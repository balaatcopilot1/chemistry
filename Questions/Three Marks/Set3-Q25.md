Chapter: Coordination Compounds

25. (a) Answer the following questions about the complexes [NiCl₄]²⁻ and [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ :
1. Write the hybridization involved in each case.
2. Which of them is the inner orbital complex and which one is the outer orbital complex ?
3. Compare their magnetic behaviour. [Atomic number : Ni = 28]
OR
(b) (i) Name two coordination compounds which are important in biological systems.
2. What is meant by chelate effect ? Give an example.
3. Why are low spin tetrahedral complexes rarely formed ?

## Answer

(a) 1. In [NiCl₄]²⁻, Ni²⁺ (3d⁸) has weak field Cl⁻ ligands, giving sp³ hybridization. In [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻, Ni²⁺ (3d⁸) has strong field CN⁻ ligands that pair 3d electrons, giving dsp² hybridization.
2. [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ uses inner 3d orbital (dsp²), so it is an inner orbital complex. [NiCl₄]²⁻ uses outer 4s and 4p orbitals (sp³), so it is a tetrahedral/outer orbital complex.
3. [NiCl₄]²⁻ has 2 unpaired 3d electrons and is paramagnetic. [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ has 0 unpaired electrons and is diamagnetic.
OR
(b) (i) Chlorophyll (coordination complex of Mg²⁺) and Haemoglobin (coordination complex of Fe²⁺).
(ii) When a di- or polydentate ligand binds to a central metal ion to form a ring structure (chelate ring), the resulting complex is significantly more stable than similar complexes with unidentate ligands. Example: [Co(en)₃]³⁺ or [Fe(ox)₃]³⁻.
(iii) In tetrahedral complexes, crystal field splitting energy (Δt) is small (Δt = 4/9 Δo) and is virtually always smaller than pairing energy (P); hence orbital splitting cannot force electron pairing to form low-spin complexes.

*Source: NCERT, Coordination Compounds*
