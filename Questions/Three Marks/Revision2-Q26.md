Chapter: Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

26. Answer the following:
(i) Why is (±)-Butan-2-ol optically inactive, though it contains a chiral carbon atom?
(ii) Why is chloroform kept in airtight dark coloured bottle?
(iii) Write the major product formed when 2-Bromobutane is heated with alcoholic KOH.

## Answer

(i) Optical Inactivity of (±)-Butan-2-ol:
(±)-Butan-2-ol is a racemic mixture containing equal amounts of (+) and (-) enantiomers. The dextrorotation caused by one isomer is exactly cancelled by an equal laevorotation of the other isomer (external compensation), rendering the mixture optically inactive.

(ii) Chloroform Storage:
Chloroform (CHCl₃) is slowly oxidized by atmospheric oxygen in the presence of light to form a highly poisonous gas called phosgene (carbonyl chloride, COCl₂). Storing it in dark, filled, airtight bottles excludes light and air to prevent oxidation:
2 CHCl₃(l) + O₂(g) --[light]--> 2 COCl₂(g)↑ + 2 HCl(g)↑

(iii) Major Product of Dehydrohalogenation:
Heating 2-bromobutane with alcoholic KOH yields But-2-ene as the major product according to Saytzeff's rule (more highly substituted alkene):
CH₃–CH(Br)–CH₂–CH₃ + KOH(alc) --[heat]--> CH₃–CH=CH–CH₃ [But-2-ene, major] + KBr + H₂O
