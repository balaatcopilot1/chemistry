28. a) Henry's law constant for CO₂ in water is 1.67 × 10⁸ Pa at 298 K. Calculate the number of moles of CO₂ in 500 ml of soda water when packed under 2.53 × 10⁵ Pa at the same temperature.
(OR)
b) A solution of glucose (M = 180 g/mol) in water has a boiling point of 100.20 °C. Calculate the freezing point of the same solution. Molar constants for water Kf and Kb are 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹ and 0.512 K kg mol⁻¹ respectively.

## Answer

(a) Given:
KH = 1.67 × 10⁸ Pa
p(CO₂) = 2.53 × 10⁵ Pa
Volume of water = 500 mL ≈ 500 g -> Moles of water, n(H₂O) = 500 / 18 = 27.78 mol

Step 1: Calculate Mole Fraction of CO₂ (x):
x(CO₂) = p(CO₂) / KH = (2.53 × 10⁵ Pa) / (1.67 × 10⁸ Pa) = 1.515 × 10⁻³

Step 2: Calculate Moles of CO₂ (n):
x(CO₂) = n / (n + 27.78) ≈ n / 27.78
n = 1.515 × 10⁻³ × 27.78 = 0.042 mol

Answer (a): 0.042 mol (or 4.2 × 10⁻² mol)

(OR)

(b) Given:
T_b = 100.20 °C -> ΔT_b = 100.20 - 100.00 = 0.20 K
K_b = 0.512 K kg mol⁻¹
K_f = 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹
T°f = 0.00 °C (273.15 K)

Step 1: Calculate Molality (m):
ΔT_b = K_b × m -> m = ΔT_b / K_b = 0.20 / 0.512 = 0.3906 mol kg⁻¹

Step 2: Calculate Freezing Point Depression (ΔT_f):
ΔT_f = K_f × m = 1.86 × 0.3906 = 0.726 K

Step 3: Calculate Freezing Point of Solution (T_f):
T_f = T°f - ΔT_f = 0.00 - 0.726 = -0.726 °C (or 272.424 K)

Answer (b): -0.726 °C (or 272.424 K)
