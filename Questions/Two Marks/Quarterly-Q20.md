Chapter: Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers

20. a) i) Explain the Reimer-Tiemann reaction.
ii) Convert benzyl alcohol to benzoic acid
(OR)
b) i) Explain Kolbe's reaction.
ii) Give reason for the higher boiling point of ethanol in comparison to methoxy methane.

## Answer

(a) (i) Reimer-Tiemann Reaction:
Treatment of phenol with chloroform in the presence of aqueous sodium hydroxide at 340 K introduces a formyl (–CHO) group at the ortho-position, forming salicylaldehyde.

Equation:
C₆H₅OH(l) + CHCl₃(l) + 3 NaOH(aq) --[340 K]--> 2-HO–C₆H₄–CHO(aq) + 3 NaCl(aq) + 2 H₂O(l)

(ii) Benzyl Alcohol to Benzoic Acid:
Oxidize benzyl alcohol with alkaline KMnO₄ upon heating, followed by acidic hydrolysis.

Equation:
C₆H₅CH₂OH(l) --[(1) KMnO₄/KOH, heat; (2) H₃O⁺]--> C₆H₅COOH(s)

(OR)

(b) (i) Kolbe's Reaction:
Phenoxide ion generated by treating phenol with NaOH undergoes electrophilic substitution with carbon dioxide at 400 K and 4–7 atm, followed by acidification to yield 2-hydroxybenzoic acid (salicylic acid).

Equation:
C₆H₅ONa(aq) + CO₂(g) --[400 K, 4-7 atm; H⁺]--> 2-HO–C₆H₄–COOH(s)

(ii) Boiling Point Difference:
Ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH) forms strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds among its molecules. Methoxymethane (CH₃OCH₃) lacks an O–H bond and exhibits only weaker dipole-dipole interactions, resulting in a lower boiling point.
