Chapter: Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

13. What are ambident nucleophiles? Explain with an example.

## Answer

Ambident nucleophiles are nucleophilic species that possess two distinct electron-donor atoms through which they can form a covalent bond with an electrophile, but bond through only one donor site in a given reaction.

Example: The cyanide ion (CN⁻) contains donor atoms at both carbon and nitrogen:
- Reaction with KCN yields alkyl cyanides (C-attack):
R–Cl(aq) + KCN(aq) → R–CN(aq) + KCl(aq)
- Reaction with AgCN yields alkyl isocyanides (N-attack):
R–Cl(aq) + AgCN(aq) → R–NC(aq) + AgCl(s)

*Source: NCERT, Haloalkanes and Haloarenes*
