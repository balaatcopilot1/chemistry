Chapter: Coordination Compounds

18. What is meant by the chelate effect? Give an example.

## Answer

(a) Definition:
The chelate effect refers to the enhanced thermodynamic stability of a coordination complex formed with didentate or polydentate ligands compared to an analogous complex formed with unidentate ligands. The binding of a chelating ligand creates ring structures containing the central metal atom, resulting in a favorable increase in system entropy (ΔS° > 0).

(b) Example:
The tris(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(III) complex ion, [Co(en)₃]³⁺, is significantly more stable than the hexaamminecobalt(III) complex ion, [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺, because ethane-1,2-diamine (en) forms three 5-membered chelate rings with Cobalt.

*Source: NCERT, Coordination Compounds*
