Chapter: Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

12. (a) Explain why alkyl halides have higher boiling points than their corresponding hydrocarbons.
(b) Determine the order of boiling points of the following alkyl halides: RF, RCl, RBr, and RI. Give a reason for your answer.

## Answer

(a) Alkyl halides are polar molecules possessing dipole-dipole interactions in addition to van der Waals forces. Hydrocarbons are non-polar and possess only weak London dispersion forces. The stronger intermolecular attraction in alkyl halides requires higher thermal energy to overcome, resulting in higher boiling points.

(b) Order of boiling points:
RF < RCl < RBr < RI

Reason: For a given alkyl group R, as the size and atomic mass of the halogen atom increase from fluorine to iodine, the magnitude of van der Waals forces increases, raising the boiling point.

*Source: NCERT, Haloalkanes and Haloarenes*
