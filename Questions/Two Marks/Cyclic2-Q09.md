Chapter: Coordination Compounds

9. Explain the 'Chelate Effect' and provide one example of a polydentate ligand that exhibits this property.

## Answer

The 'Chelate Effect' refers to the enhanced thermodynamic stability of a coordination complex formed by a didentate or polydentate ligand compared to an analogous complex formed with unidentate ligands. When a chelate ligand binds to a central metal ion, it forms one or more five- or six-membered ring structures, which results in a favorable increase in entropy (ΔS° > 0).

Example of a polydentate ligand exhibiting chelate effect:
Ethane-1,2-diamine (en) or Ethylenediaminetetraacetate ion (EDTA⁴⁻).

Complex Example: [Co(en)₃]³⁺ is significantly more stable than [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ due to the chelate effect.
