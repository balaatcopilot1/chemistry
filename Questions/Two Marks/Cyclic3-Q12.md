Chapter: Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids

12. Give reasons for the following:
(i) Why is there a significant difference in the boiling points of butanal and butanol?
(ii) Distinguish between Acetaldehyde and benzaldehyde.

## Answer

(i) Butanol (CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH) molecules are associated through strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding due to the polar –OH group. Butanal (CH₃CH₂CH₂CHO) possesses only dipole-dipole interactions, which are weaker than hydrogen bonds. Consequently, butanol has a significantly higher boiling point than butanal.

(ii) Distinguish test: Iodoform Test
Acetaldehyde (CH₃CHO) contains a CH₃C=O group and gives a yellow precipitate of iodoform (CHI₃) when warmed with I₂ and NaOH(aq). Benzaldehyde (C₆H₅CHO) does not contain a CH₃C=O group and does not give the iodoform test.

Reaction for Acetaldehyde:
CH₃CHO(aq) + 3 I₂(aq) + 4 NaOH(aq) → CHI₃(s) [yellow ppt] + HCOONa(aq) + 3 NaI(aq) + 3 H₂O(l)
