Chapter: Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

20. When haloalkanes with β-hydrogen atom are boiled with alcoholic solution of KOH, they undergo elimination of hydrogen halide resulting in the formation of alkenes. These reactions are called β-elimination reactions or dehydrohalogenation reactions. These reactions follow Saytzeff's rule. Substitution and elimination reactions often compete with each other. Mostly bases behave as nucleophiles and therefore can engage in substitution or elimination reactions depending upon the alkyl halide and the reaction conditions.

20.a. What happens when ethyl chloride is treated with aqueous KOH ?
20.b. Out of 2-bromopentane, 2-bromo-2-methylbutane, and 1-bromopentane, which compound is most reactive towards elimination reaction and why?
20.c. Predict the order of reactivity of the following compounds in SN1 reaction :
C₆H₅CH₂Br, C₆H₅C(CH₃)(C₆H₅)Br, C₆H₅CH(C₆H₅)Br, C₆H₅CH(CH₃)Br
20.d. Which alkyl halide from the following pair is chiral and undergoes faster SN2 reaction ?
(i) 2-bromobutane
(ii) 1-bromobutane

![Diagram](Questions/images/Cyclic1-Q20.png)
The diagram shows the skeletal structures of (i) 2-bromobutane and (ii) 1-bromobutane.

## Answer

(a) When ethyl chloride is treated with aqueous KOH, it undergoes nucleophilic substitution to produce ethanol.
CH₃CH₂Cl(aq) + KOH(aq) → CH₃CH₂OH(aq) + KCl(aq)

(b) 2-Bromo-2-methylbutane is most reactive towards elimination. Being a tertiary alkyl halide, it dehydrohalogenates to yield a highly substituted, stable alkene (2-methylbut-2-ene) in accordance with Saytzeff's rule.

(c) Reactivity order in SN1 reaction:
C₆H₅C(CH₃)(C₆H₅)Br > C₆H₅CH(C₆H₅)Br > C₆H₅CH(CH₃)Br > C₆H₅CH₂Br
Reason: SN1 reactivity depends on carbocation stability. C₆H₅C(CH₃)(C₆H₅)Br forms a tertiary benzylic carbocation stabilized by resonance with two phenyl rings and hyperconjugation with the methyl group.

(d) Chiral compound: 2-Bromobutane (i) is chiral because its C-2 carbon is bonded to four different groups (–H, –Br, –CH₃, –CH₂CH₃).
Faster SN2 reaction: 1-Bromobutane (ii) undergoes faster SN2 reaction because it is a primary alkyl halide experiencing minimal steric hindrance.

*Source: NCERT, Haloalkanes and Haloarenes*
