Chapter: Biomolecules

30. Carbohydrates are optically active polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones or the compounds which produce such units on hydrolysis. They have been broadly classified into three groups — monosaccharides, oligosaccharides and polysaccharides. The carbohydrates may also be classified as either reducing or non-reducing sugars. An important monosaccharide glucose is an aldohexose and is correctly named as D(+)–glucose. It was assigned the open structure on the basis of its reactions with HI, NH₂OH, Br₂ water, (CH₃CO)₂O and nitric acid.

Answer the following questions :
(a) How will you explain the following ?
(1) Presence of carbonyl group in glucose.
(2) Presence of five – OH groups in glucose attached to different carbon atoms. [2 Marks]
(b) What type of carbohydrates are called reducing sugars ? [1 Mark]
(c) (i) In D(+)–glucose, what does the letter 'D' and sign '(+)' represent ? [1 Mark]
OR
(c) (ii) Draw the six-membered ring structure of α–D(+)–glucose. [1 Mark]

## Answer

(a) (1) Glucose reacts with hydroxylamine (NH₂OH) to form an oxime, and adds a molecule of hydrogen cyanide (HCN) to form a cyanohydrin, confirming the presence of a carbonyl group (>C=O).
(2) Acetylation of glucose with acetic anhydride yields glucose pentaacetate, proving the presence of five hydroxyl (–OH) groups; since glucose is stable, each –OH group must be attached to a different carbon atom.

(b) Carbohydrates that reduce Fehling's solution and Tollens' reagent due to the presence of a free aldehydic or ketonic carbonyl group (or hemiacetal group) are called reducing sugars (e.g. glucose, fructose, lactose).

(c) (i) The letter 'D' indicates the absolute configuration relative to D-glyceraldehyde (–OH on C-5 is on the right in Fischer projection), while the sign '(+)' indicates that the compound is dextrorotatory.
OR
(c) (ii) α–D(+)–glucopyranose is a six-membered pyranose ring where C-1 (anomeric carbon) has the –OH group in the alpha (downward) orientation.
