Chapter: Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers

20. Read the following passage and answer the questions:
The C–O bond in ethers can be cleaved by hydrogen halides (HX). The reaction of tertiary alkyl ethers with HI typically follows an SN1 mechanism. Because the tertiary carbocation is highly stable, the halide ion (I⁻) preferentially attacks the tertiary carbon. In contrast, with primary alkyl groups, the I⁻ ion attacks the smaller, less hindered alkyl group via an SN2 mechanism.

20.a. Write the products of the reaction: CH₃–O–C(CH₃)₃ + HI → ?
20.b. Why does the reaction of anisole with HI yield phenol and methyl iodide rather than iodobenzene and methanol?
20.c. Identify the mechanism (SN1 or SN2) followed when diethyl ether reacts with HI.
20.d. Which is more reactive toward ether cleavage: HBr or HI? Give a reason.

## Answer

(a) Products of the reaction:
CH₃–O–C(CH₃)₃ + HI → CH₃–OH + (CH₃)₃C–I
Products are methanol and tert-butyl iodide. (The reaction proceeds via SN1 forming stable tertiary carbocation (CH₃)₃C⁺ which combines with I⁻).

(b) In anisole (C₆H₅–O–CH₃), the C(aryl)–O bond has partial double bond character due to resonance between the lone pair of oxygen and the benzene ring, making it stronger than the C(alkyl)–O bond. Additionally, the sp²-hybridized phenyl carbon resists nucleophilic substitution. Thus, cleavage occurs at the alkyl C–O bond, yielding phenol (C₆H₅OH) and methyl iodide (CH₃I).

(c) Mechanism for diethyl ether with HI:
SN2 mechanism. Since both alkyl groups are primary (ethyl groups), the iodide ion attacks the less sterically hindered protonated ether species via a bimolecular nucleophilic substitution pathway.

(d) HI is more reactive than HBr toward ether cleavage.
Reason: HI is a stronger acid than HBr (due to lower H–I bond dissociation energy) and I⁻ is a stronger nucleophile than Br⁻ due to its larger size and higher polarizability.
