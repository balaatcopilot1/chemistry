Chapter: Electrochemistry

20. The following question (Q. No. 20) is a case-based question.
Read the passage carefully and answer the questions that following:

Metallic conductance involves movement of electrons where as electrolytic conductance involves movement of ions. Specific conductance increases with increase in concentration where as Λm (molar conductivity) decreases with increase in concentration. Electrolytic cell converts electrical energy into chemical energy which is used in electrolysis. Amount of products formed are decided with the help of Faraday's laws of Electrolysis. Kohlrausch law helps to determine limiting molar conductivity of weak electrolyte, their degree of ionisation (α) and their dissociation constants.

a) How is the variation of molar conductance with concentration of strong electrolyte expressed?
b) A 0.01M solution of MgCl₂ is diluted by adding water. What will happen to its conductivity and molar conductivity?
c) Express the relation between conductivity and molar conductivity of a solution.
d) X and Y are two electrolytes. On dilution molar conductivity of X increases 2.5 times while that of Y increases 25 times. Which of the two is a weak electrolyte and why?

## Answer

(a) The variation of molar conductivity (Λm) with concentration for a strong electrolyte is expressed by the Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation:
Λm = Λm° - A c¹/²
Where Λm° is limiting molar conductivity, c is concentration, and A is a constant depending on solvent and temperature.

(b) On dilution of 0.01M MgCl₂ solution:
- Specific Conductivity (κ) decreases because the number of current-carrying ions per unit volume decreases.
- Molar Conductivity (Λm) increases because interionic attractions decrease and total volume containing one mole of electrolyte increases.

(c) Relation between conductivity (κ) and molar conductivity (Λm):
Λm = (κ × 1000) / c
Where κ is conductivity in S cm⁻¹, c is molar concentration in mol L⁻¹, and Λm is in S cm² mol⁻¹.

(d) Y is a weak electrolyte.
Reason: Weak electrolytes undergo incomplete dissociation at higher concentrations. Upon dilution, their degree of dissociation (α) increases sharply according to Ostwald's dilution law, causing a steep (25-fold) increase in molar conductivity. Strong electrolytes (X) are already fully dissociated, so dilution increases Λm only slightly (2.5-fold) due to reduced interionic attraction.
