Chemical kinetics deals with the rate of chemical reactions, how fast reactants get used up or how fast products are formed in the reaction. Different chemical reactions have different speeds. Rate of a reaction depends upon concentration of reactants, temperature, pressure especially in gaseous reactions and presence of catalyst. Chemical reaction takes place as a result of collision between reacting molecules. The rate of reaction does not depend upon the total number of collisions rather it depends upon the number of effective collisions. In a redox reaction, if E°cell is +ve, ΔG° will be -ve and 'K' equilibrium constant will be high i.e. products formed will be more than the reactants.

(A) The rate constant for the first order decomposition of H₂O₂ is given by the following equation: log k = 14.34 - 1.25 × 10⁴ K/T. Calculate Ea for the reaction.
(B) In a reaction, 2A → Products, the concentration of A decreases from 0.5 mol L⁻¹ to 0.4 mol L⁻¹ in 10 minutes. Calculate the rate during this interval?
(C) What is meant by activation energy? What does e⁻ᴱᵃ/ᴿᵀ represent?
(D) What is collision frequency?

## Answer

(a) Calculation of Eₐ:
Arrhenius equation: log k = log A - Eₐ / (2.303 R T)
Eₐ / (2.303 R) = 1.25 × 10⁴ K
Eₐ = 1.25 × 10⁴ K × 2.303 × 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ = 239,341 J mol⁻¹ = 239.34 kJ mol⁻¹

(b) Calculation of Average Rate:
Average Rate = - Δ[A] / (2 Δt) = - (0.4 - 0.5) / (2 × 10 min) = 0.1 / 20 = 0.005 mol L⁻¹ min⁻¹

(c) Activation Energy & Exponential Factor:
Activation energy (Eₐ) is the minimum energy threshold reactant molecules must possess to form the activated complex.
The factor e⁻ᴱᵃ/ᴿᵀ represents the fraction of molecules having energy equal to or greater than activation energy.

(d) Collision Frequency:
Collision frequency (Z) is the number of collisions occurring per second per unit volume of the reaction mixture.
