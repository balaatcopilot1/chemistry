Chapter: Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

22. Draw the structure of major monohalo product for each of the following reactions
(i) Cyclohexanol + SOCl₂ →
(ii) 1-Methylcyclohexene + HI →
(iii) Methylbenzene + Br₂ --[Heat / UV light]-->
(iv) 4-Hydroxybenzyl alcohol + HCl --[Heat]-->
(v) CH₃CH₂Br + NaI →

![Diagram](Questions/images/Cyclic1-Q22.png)
The diagram shows five organic reaction schemes with reactants and conditions.

## Answer

(i) Chlorocyclohexane
Structure: A six-membered cyclohexane ring attached to a chlorine atom (C₆H₁₁Cl).
Explanation: Thionyl chloride (SOCl₂) converts the hydroxyl group of cyclohexanol to a chloride with gaseous SO₂ and HCl byproducts.

(ii) 1-Iodo-1-methylcyclohexane
Structure: A cyclohexane ring with both a methyl group (–CH₃) and an iodine atom (–I) attached to the same tertiary carbon atom.
Explanation: Addition of HI across the double bond follows Markovnikov's rule via the more stable tertiary carbocation intermediate.

(iii) Benzyl bromide (Bromomethylbenzene)
Structure: C₆H₅–CH₂Br
Explanation: Free-radical bromination under heat and UV light selectively occurs at the benzylic position.

(iv) 4-(Chloromethyl)phenol
Structure: 4-HO–C₆H₄–CH₂Cl
Explanation: The alcoholic –CH₂OH group undergoes nucleophilic substitution with HCl to form –CH₂Cl, while the phenolic –OH group remains unreacted due to resonance double-bond character.

(v) Ethyl iodide (Iodoethane)
Structure: CH₃–CH₂–I
Explanation: Halogen exchange (Finkelstein reaction) converts ethyl bromide to ethyl iodide when treated with NaI in acetone.

*Source: NCERT, Haloalkanes and Haloarenes*
