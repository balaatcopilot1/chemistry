Chapter: Coordination Compounds

21. (a) According to Crystal Field Theory, how does the d-orbital splitting differ between an octahedral and a tetrahedral field? (Draw the energy diagrams).
(b) Calculate the Crystal Field Stabilization Energy (CFSE) for a d⁵ ion in an octahedral field with a weak field ligand.
(c) Why are most transition metal complexes colored?

## Answer

(a) d-Orbital Splitting Difference:
In an octahedral field, ligands approach along the Cartesian axes. The dx²-y² and dz² orbitals (eg set) experience greater repulsion and rise in energy, while dxy, dyz, dzx (t₂g set) decrease in energy. Splitting energy is Δ⒪ (eg is 0.6 Δ⒪ above, t₂g is 0.4 Δ⒪ below).
In a tetrahedral field, ligands approach between the axes. The dxy, dyz, dzx orbitals (t₂ set) experience higher repulsion and rise in energy, while dx²-y² and dz² (e set) decrease. Splitting energy is Δₜ = (4/9) Δ⒪.

(b) CFSE Calculation for d⁵ ion in weak octahedral field:
For a weak field ligand, Δ⒪ < P (pairing energy). High spin configuration is t₂g³ eg².
CFSE = [-0.4 × (number of electrons in t₂g) + 0.6 × (number of electrons in eg)] Δ⒪
CFSE = [-0.4 × 3 + 0.6 × 2] Δ⒪ = [-1.2 + 1.2] Δ⒪ = 0 Δ⒪

(c) Reason for color in transition metal complexes:
In the presence of ligands, d-orbitals split into different energy levels. When white light falls on the complex, an electron absorbs light of a specific wavelength corresponding to Δ⒪ and gets excited from a lower d-orbital to a higher d-orbital (d-d transition). The transmitted light exhibits the complementary color to the absorbed light.
