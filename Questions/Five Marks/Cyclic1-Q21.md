Chapter: Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

21. (i) How the following conversions can be carried out?
(a) Propene to propan-1-ol
(b) Aniline to phenyl isocyanide
(c) Benzene to diphenyl
(ii) Give reasons for the following:
(a) (±) 2 - Butanol is optically inactive.
(b) Why is the carbocation formed during an electrophilic substitution reaction of benzene relatively stable?

## Answer

(i) Conversions:

(a) Propene to propan-1-ol:
Step 1: Treat propene with HBr in the presence of peroxide to obtain 1-bromopropane via anti-Markovnikov addition.
CH₃–CH=CH₂ + HBr --[peroxide]--> CH₃–CH₂–CH₂Br
Step 2: Hydrolyze 1-bromopropane with aqueous KOH to yield propan-1-ol.
CH₃–CH₂–CH₂Br + KOH(aq) → CH₃–CH₂–CH₂OH + KBr

(b) Aniline to phenyl isocyanide:
Heat aniline with chloroform and ethanolic KOH (Carbylamine reaction).
C₆H₅–NH₂ + CHCl₃ + 3 KOH(alc) --[heat]--> C₆H₅–NC + 3 KCl + 3 H₂O

(c) Benzene to diphenyl:
Step 1: Brominate benzene with Br₂ in the presence of FeBr₃ to form bromobenzene.
C₆H₆ + Br₂ --[FeBr₃]--> C₆H₅–Br + HBr
Step 2: React bromobenzene with sodium metal in dry ether (Fittig reaction) to yield diphenyl.
2 C₆H₅–Br + 2 Na --[dry ether]--> C₆H₅–C₆H₅ + 2 NaBr

(ii) Reasons:

(a) (±) 2-Butanol is a racemic mixture composed of equal amounts of dextrorotatory (+) and laevorotatory (–) enantiomers. The optical rotation caused by one enantiomer is cancelled by an equal and opposite rotation caused by the other enantiomer (external compensation), rendering the mixture optically inactive.

(b) The carbocation intermediate (arenium ion or σ-complex) formed during electrophilic aromatic substitution of benzene is stabilized by resonance, with the positive charge delocalized over the ortho and para positions of the ring.

*Source: NCERT, Haloalkanes and Haloarenes*
