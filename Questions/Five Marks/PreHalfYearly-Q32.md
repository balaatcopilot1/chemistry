Chapter: Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

32. A. i) How is alkene converted to vic-dibromide?
(ii) Arrange the following in the increasing order of densities: CH₂Cl₂, CHCl₃, CCl₄
(iii) Convert: 1-Bromobutane to 2-bromobutane
(iv) Arrange the compounds of each set in the order of reactivity towards SN2 displacement:
(a) 2-Bromo-2-methylbutane, 1-Bromopentane, 2-Bromopentane.
(b) 1-Bromo-3-methylbutane, 2-Bromo-2-methylbutane, 2-Bromo-3-methylbutane.
OR
B. (i) How is phenol prepared from chlorobenzene? Write the chemical equations and conditions.
(ii) Explain why phenol is more acidic than ethanol?
(iii) Write the equations for the reaction of diethyl ether with excess HI on heating.
(iv) Describe the mechanism of acid-catalysed dehydration of ethanol to ethene.

## Answer

A. (i) Addition of Br₂ in CCl₄ to an alkene yields a vicinal dibromide (reddish-brown bromine color is discharged):
CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ --[CCl₄]--> CH₂Br–CH₂Br (1,2-dibromoethane)

(ii) Order of densities: CH₂Cl₂ < CHCl₃ < CCl₄ (density increases with increase in mass and number of halogen atoms).

(iii) Convert 1-bromobutane to 2-bromobutane:
Step 1: Dehydrohalogenate with alcoholic KOH to get But-1-ene:
CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂Br + KOH(alc) --[heat]--> CH₃CH₂CH=CH₂ + KBr + H₂O
Step 2: Add HBr according to Markovnikov's rule to obtain 2-bromobutane:
CH₃CH₂CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃CH₂CH(Br)CH₃

(iv) SN2 Reactivity Order (1° > 2° > 3° due to steric hindrance):
(a) 2-Bromo-2-methylbutane (3°) < 2-Bromopentane (2°) < 1-Bromopentane (1°)
(b) 2-Bromo-2-methylbutane (3°) < 2-Bromo-3-methylbutane (2°) < 1-Bromo-3-methylbutane (1°)

OR

B. (i) Dow's Process: Chlorobenzene is fused with NaOH at 623 K and 300 atm to form sodium phenoxide, followed by acidification:
C₆H₅Cl + 2 NaOH --[623 K, 300 atm]--> C₆H₅ONa + NaCl + H₂O
C₆H₅ONa + HCl → C₆H₅OH + NaCl

(ii) Phenol ionizes to phenoxide ion (C₆H₅O⁻) where negative charge is delocalized over benzene ring by resonance. Ethoxide ion (C₂H₅O⁻) lacks resonance and is destabilized by electron-donating +I ethyl group.

(iii) Diethyl ether with excess HI:
C₂H₅–O–C₂H₅ + 2 HI --[heat]--> 2 C₂H₅I + H₂O

(iv) Mechanism of Acid-Catalysed Dehydration of Ethanol (443 K):
Step 1: Protonation of alcohol:
CH₃CH₂OH + H⁺ ⇌ CH₃CH₂O⁺H₂
Step 2: Formation of carbocation (slow rate-determining step):
CH₃CH₂O⁺H₂ ⇌ CH₃CH₂⁺ + H₂O
Step 3: Elimination of proton to form ethene:
CH₃CH₂⁺ → CH₂=CH₂ + H⁺

*Source: NCERT, Haloalkanes and Haloarenes*
