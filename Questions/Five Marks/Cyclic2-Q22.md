Chapter: Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers

22. An organic compound (A) with molecular formula C₃H₈O produces a prompt turbidity with Lucas reagent. When vapours of (A) are passed over heated Copper at 573 K, it gives compound (B). Compound (B) does not reduce Tollens' reagent but forms a yellow precipitate with I₂/NaOH.
(a) Identify A and B and write their IUPAC names.
(b) Write the chemical equation for the reaction of (A) with PCl₅.
(c) Explain why phenol is more acidic than compound (A).

## Answer

(a) Identification of A and B:
- Compound (A) produces prompt turbidity with Lucas reagent and has formula C₃H₈O, so it is a secondary alcohol: Propan-2-ol, CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₃.
- Dehydration/dehydrogenation of propan-2-ol over Cu at 573 K yields propanone (acetone), CH₃–CO–CH₃, which is compound (B). Propanone does not reduce Tollens' reagent but gives positive iodoform test (yellow precipitate of CHI₃ with I₂/NaOH).

IUPAC Names:
- Compound (A): Propan-2-ol
- Compound (B): Propanone

(b) Chemical Equation of (A) with PCl₅:
CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₃(l) + PCl₅(s) → CH₃–CH(Cl)–CH₃(l) + POCl₃(l) + HCl(g)
(Product is 2-chloropropane).

(c) Reason why phenol is more acidic than compound (A):
Phenol ionizes to form phenoxide ion (C₆H₅O⁻). The negative charge on oxygen in phenoxide is delocalized over the aromatic ring through resonance, making phenoxide ion highly stable. In contrast, ionization of propan-2-ol gives alkoxide ion (CH₃–CHO⁻–CH₃), where the negative charge is localized on oxygen and destabilized by the +I electron-donating effect of two methyl groups. Thus, phenol loses H⁺ much more readily than propan-2-ol.
