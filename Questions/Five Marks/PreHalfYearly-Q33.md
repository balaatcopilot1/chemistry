Chapter: Amines

33. A. i) Arrange the following:
a) In increasing order of basic character: C₆H₅NH₂, CH₃NH₂, (C₂H₅)₂NH, C₆H₅N(CH₃)₂
b) In increasing order of boiling point: C₂H₅OH, (CH₃)₂NH, C₂H₅NH₂
ii) Write the reaction involved when:
a) aniline reacts with chloroform and ethanolic potassium hydroxide
b) Gabriel phthalimide synthesis
iii) How can you convert aniline to iodobenzene?
OR
B. i) Convert the following transformations:
ii) A compound Z with molecular formula C₃H₉N reacts with C₆H₅SO₂Cl to give a solid, insoluble in alkali. Identify Z.
iii) Complete the following reactions:
a) C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ --[(i) HBF₄ / (ii) NaNO₂/Cu/Heat]--> ?
b) C₆H₅NH₂ + (CH3CO)₂O --[pyridine]--> ?
c) Ammonolysis is not preferred for preparation of amines. Why?

## Answer

A. (i) (a) Basic Character: C₆H₅NH₂ < C₆H₅N(CH₃)₂ < CH₃NH₂ < (C₂H₅)₂NH
(b) Boiling Point: (CH₃)₂NH < C₂H₅NH₂ < C₂H₅OH

(ii) (a) Carbylamine Reaction:
C₆H₅NH₂ + CHCl₃ + 3 KOH(alc) --[heat]--> C₆H₅NC + 3 KCl + 3 H₂O
(b) Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis:
Phthalimide + KOH(alc) → Potassium phthalimide --[R–X]--> N-alkylphthalimide --[NaOH(aq)]--> R–NH₂ + Sodium phthalate.

(iii) Aniline to Iodobenzene:
C₆H₅NH₂ + NaNO₂ + 2 HCl --[273–278 K]--> C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + NaCl + 2 H₂O
C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + KI --[warm]--> C₆H₅I + N₂↑ + KCl

OR

B. (i) Transformations: Aniline to Benzonitrile: C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + CuCN → C₆H₅CN + N₂↑ + KCl.

(ii) Identification of Z:
Formula C₃H₉N reacting with Hinsberg reagent (C₆H₅SO₂Cl) to give an alkali-insoluble product indicates a secondary amine:
Z = N-Ethylethanamine [(C₂H₅)₂NH] or N-Methylpropan-2-amine.

(iii) Reactions:
(a) C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ --[(i) HBF₄, (ii) NaNO₂/Cu, heat]--> C₆H₅NO₂ (Nitrobenzene) + N₂ + NaBF₄
(b) C₆H₅NH₂ + (CH₃CO)₂O --[pyridine]--> C₆H₅NHCOCH₃ (Acetanilide) + CH₃COOH
(c) Ammonolysis of alkyl halides produces a mixture of primary, secondary, tertiary amines and quaternary ammonium salts, which are difficult to separate into pure components.
